FinOps Is Not Optional in 2026 — It's Infrastructure

October 5, 2024

FinOps started as a cloud cost management practice. In 2026, it's evolved into something bigger: managing technology value across cloud, SaaS, licensing, data center, and AI spend. The 2026 State of FinOps report shows 98% of practitioners now manage AI spend, up from 31% two years ago. Another 90% manage SaaS or plan to. 64% manage software licensing. 57% manage private cloud. 48% manage data center costs. This isn't just about saving money anymore — it's about making sure every technology dollar produces measurable value.

The Shift

Three things changed between 2024 and 2026. First, AI spend became the top priority. AI investment isn't slowing down. Many FinOps teams are being asked to self-fund AI investments through optimization savings — cut waste in traditional cloud spend to fund GPU instances and AI services. This means the traditional FinOps work (rightsizing instances, buying reserved capacity, eliminating waste) directly enables strategic investments.

Second, FinOps expanded beyond cloud. The FinOps Open Cost and Usage Specification (FOCUS) is the standard for normalizing cost data across cloud providers, SaaS subscriptions, licensing agreements, and data center operations. This lets organizations do comparative analysis rather than managing each cost silo separately.

Third, the practice moved left into engineering workflows. Instead of reviewing costs after deployment, platforms now show cost estimates before you provision. Cost visibility moves from "invoice review at the end of the month" to "pre-deployment guardrail that prevents overspend." 52% of engineering leaders say the disconnect between FinOps and developers wastes cloud spend. The fix is embedding cost awareness into the tools developers already use — CI/CD pipelines, deployment manifests, and dashboards.

What Works

The most successful FinOps practices share common patterns: executive alignment with VP+ support (practitioners with this have 2-4x more influence over technology decisions), automation of rate optimization and workload placement, developer-facing FinOps that gives developers budgets and dashboards instead of blame, and FOCUS adoption for consistent cost data across providers and services.

Getting Started in 2026

If you don't have a FinOps practice yet, start with visibility. You can't optimize what you can't see. Get cost data from all your major providers into one place. Tag resources consistently. Allocate costs to teams based on resource tags and usage metrics. Then start optimizing the biggest line items. The goal isn't to cut costs to zero — it's to make sure every dollar you spend on technology creates more value than it costs. That's a FinOps mindset that scales beyond cloud into everything your organization buys.
